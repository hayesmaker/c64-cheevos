## Panther (Commodore 64)

**Rescue mission engaged. Fly low, shoot straight and bring the survivors home.**

*Panther* is a 1986 Mastertronic budget shoot 'em up for the Commodore 64, designed and coded by **Peter Adams** with a soundtrack by **David Whittaker**. Aliens have overrun the city of Xenon, and you are the last pilot left who can fly a Panther ground attack craft. Fly across the scrolling isometric landscape, rescue the people left behind and get them to the spaceport before the waves of alien attackers bring you down.

**Key Features:**

*   **Isometric Action:** The ground scrolls diagonally beneath you, from the desert base across a lake with oil platforms to the city and its skyscrapers.
*   **Attack Waves:** Alpha, Beta, Gamma and more. Each wave is announced on the cockpit display as it launches.
*   **Rescue Missions:** Land by the bunkers to pick up survivors, then land again at the end of your run to get them to safety for a big bonus.
*   **Fly Under Radar:** Missile batteries hide among the city's buildings. Drop below their radar and fly around the skyscrapers, not over them.
*   **Phoenix Shields:** Five shields repair your Panther when it is hit.
*   **David Whittaker Soundtrack:** Music from one of the C64's best known composers.

**Gameplay:**

Shoot down the attackers, collect survivors and land to drop them off. Every survivor you bring home is worth far more than an enemy kill, and completing a drop-off can earn you an extra Phoenix shield. Crash or get shot down and you lose a life. The game ends when your last life is gone.

**Why Play *Panther*?**

It's a fast, colourful budget blaster with a strong risk and reward hook. Do you play it safe and pick off the enemy waves, or go after the survivors for the big points?
